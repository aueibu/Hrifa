Live Series

Any set of notes sits inside some harmonic series. This page finds that series and its fundamental, then follows the fundamental as you add and remove notes. A note that already fits the series leaves the fundamental where it is. A note that does not fit pulls it down, sometimes by octaves. The fundamental can be heard as a drone (raised by octaves) or, when it falls below hearing, as a pulse: each note becomes a stream of pulses, and all the streams meet again once per fundamental cycle.

Beats, drawn

8 s window · the solid line is now · dashed: two beats peak together · click a lane to look closer

One row per note against its exact harmonic, plus a row for each pair of listened notes' meeting partials; each row has its own listen. The band is the loudness of the two tones together, computed from when they started and every retune, so it matches what you hear; a row that is off is flat. Closer view: a strobe view, four cycles of each tone frozen on the first tone; the second tone slides one full cycle per beat, and the sum swells when they line up and cancels when they are opposite. The tones are hundreds of cycles per second, so this view is not to real time.

Phasing, drawn

5 min window, 1 min back · the solid line is now · tall = the two beats peak together · click a row to look closer

One row per pair of notes; its listen turns both notes' listen on or off. For each beat of the slower note, a bar shows how close the other note's nearest beat is: full height when they peak together, flat at 120 ms or more apart; bars within 30 ms are in the marker color. The beats carry no accents, so this is the phasing you can hear: some pairs stay together throughout, others drift apart for minutes and come back. Closer view: both beat streams as rows of ticks over about two patterns around now; ticks that fall together are marked.
