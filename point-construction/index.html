Construction Rules

Circle Inversion

Prototype: hold a circle or arc constant and invert everything else through it — P′ = O + (r²/|P−O|²)(P−O), using its own center and radius (an arc's circle is whatever passes through its j/apex/k — the whole circle is pointwise fixed, not just the visible arc portion). Lines/arcs not through it become arcs; circles become circles; anything through it maps to a line.

Nothing held constant — construction shown as-is.

Inspector

Hover a line, arc, circle, or point to see its construction stats.
Two-phase model: (1) every point's role is decided first, all at once — line/arc counts are forced even, flipping any odd one out to a plain point so nothing gets pre-assigned a role it can never execute. (2) P0→Pn then executes: circle radius = da²/db from its fixed center; line/arc points pair only with other same-role points (ranked by the Nth-closest control); the arc apex m is whichever existing point lands nearest the ratio-placed point l.
Guide line numbers: 1 = closest reference point, 2 = next-closest. AB/AC mark the two distances feeding a ratio calculation (see the endpoint labeled in each formula).
P′ = O + (r²/|P−O|²)(P−O), using the constant circle's own center O and radius r. Lines/arcs not through it become arcs (via the circle through O and their inverted endpoints); circles become circles; anything through the constant maps to a line.
