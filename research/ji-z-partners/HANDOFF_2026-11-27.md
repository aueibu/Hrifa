## 0. TL;DR

- **Object of study.** JI pitch sets as finite point sets in the prime-exponent lattice Z^n (one axis per odd prime 3, 5, 7, …; octaves ignored). A **Z-pair** = two sets P, Q with the same multiset of intervals (differences, up to sign) where Q is not a translate or a mirror (−P + t) of P. ("Homometric sets" in the math literature.) Scored by the **intervallic odd limit q**: the largest odd limit (max of numerator, denominator after removing 2s) over all intervals inside a set.
- **Goal.** Complete, proven catalogues of JI Z-pairs by note count, prime limit and q, plus their structure, for musical use (the Ratio Rhythm Lab / Harmonic Field Lab in `shell/`). The method that works best for small note counts: classify the finitely many **universal types ("shapes")** for each note count k; placing the shapes then gives the exact list at any limit and q.
- **Where it stands.** k = 6, 7: all shapes proven. k = 8: all 2-D shapes proven (4; 1-D lines not enumerated). **k = 9: shapes of dimension ≥ 3 proven (exactly 2); the 2-D list is open — 9 shapes known (a lower bound), 5 of them found on 2026-11-27.**
- **The blocker.** The exact 2-D search for k = 9 (`typeflats-rs 9 --min-dim 2`) was run for 20 h on 48 cores and stopped at 2% (3,978 of 200,026 frontier flats): projected ~41 more days, ~$1,200. It is resumable. A sound prune ("skip nodes inside a known shape") was tested and saves < 1.5%. **The open problem: find a way to settle (or extend far) the 2-D 9-note list for much less compute.** Section 6 lists the candidate directions.

## 2. The mathematics you need (compressed)

- **Polynomials.** Set S ↦ P_S(x) = Σ_{v∈S} x^v (Laurent, one variable per odd prime). Interval content = P·P\* (P\*(x) = P(1/x)). Two sets are homometric ⇔ P_S P_S\* = P_T P_T\*.
- **Factor law (Rosenblatt–Seymour 1982).** Homometric ⇔ P = U·V, Q = ±x^t U·V\* (some factors reversed). Empirically almost every proven family is **exactly two irreducible factors, one flipped**; one is usually a plain 3-note chord. Newton polytopes: hull(UV) = hull(U) + hull(V) (bounds factor width by set width). Weight: U(1)V(1) = k. "Loose" two-factor law = R–S (not a finding); the "strict" form (one factor a plain chord) is false (residue).
- **Universal types (the frontier tool).** Coordinates p_1..p_{k−1}, q_1..q_{k−1} (p_0 = q_0 = 0) in R^d. A Z-pair picks a matching "interval u of P = ±interval v of Q" for every u; each matching is a set of linear equations, identical on every prime axis, so it defines a **flat** of a hyperplane arrangement in R^{2(k−1)}. Every pair with that matching, in any lattice, is an integer linear image of the flat's generic pair. Finitely many matchings ⇒ finitely many **type flats**; the **maximal** ones (up to relabeling notes, mirroring either set, swapping P/Q) are the **shapes**. A flat is **bad** (pruned, inherited by sub-flats) if two notes of a set coincide or Q = ±P + t. **Dimension** of a shape = dim of its flat (2-D shapes are realized with two generator ratios a, b).
- **q of a placed 2-D shape.** Notes at x·a + y·b ⇒ q = max over interval vectors (x, y) of odd(a^x b^y). **Lower bound for any realization:** if an interval vector is m·(primitive), q ≥ 3^m. First q of a shape = min over integer maps (a, b) with a, b independent.
- **Containment.** Shape A "lies inside" B iff A's generic pair is a linear image of B's (`type_image.image(B, A)`); a type flat inside another is not maximal. Any search node inside a known type flat can be skipped soundly (sub-flats inherit containment) — but see §5.4: it does not help.

## 4. The k = 9 min-dim 2 search in detail (the thing that stalled)

- Binary `typeflats-rs` (Rust, std + optional mimalloc). Command (`tables/shapes9/k9d2.sh`, VM `~/zruns/k9d2.sh`):
  `typeflats-rs 9 --threads 48 --frontier 200000 --min-dim 2 --fast --canon2 --dedup-min-dim 5 --sample-out … --sample-every 10000000 --state ~/zruns/types9d2/state --out ~/zruns/types9d2/types9.json`
  - `--frontier F`: BFS until F flats, saved to `state/frontier.txt`; then threads take frontier flats one by one (DFS each). `state/done.txt`: `T <flat> <basis>` (type flats found) then `D <flat> <nodes> <bad>` per finished flat. Rerunning the same command resumes (in-flight flats redone).
  - `--min-dim 2`: never descend below dimension 2 (so 1-D types are not found). `--fast` O(m²) child check; `--canon2` allocation-light canonical key with twin pruning; `--dedup-min-dim 5`: dedup (class table) only for flats of dim ≥ 5 (memory; dim 3–4 flats are re-explored when reached by different parents); `--summarize DONE_FILE` (added 2026-11-27): one example per canon class of a stopped run's type flats.
  - Final step (only at completion): maximal filter + one representative per class → `types9.json`.
- **Numbers.** 20 h on n4d-standard-48 (~740–760 k nodes/s), 54.3 B nodes, 3,978/200,026 frontier flats. Per frontier flat: median 1 node (~90% are duplicates), mean ~13.3 M nodes, max 456 M; cost per block of 100 ids did not fall with the id (0–3,600). Projection ~2.6 T nodes left ⇒ ~41 days at 48 cores (~$1,200 spot); n4d-96 ~20 days, ~$1,000. Sampled good nodes by dim: 2: 94, 3: 2,703, 4: 2,439, 5: 73 (dims 3–4 ≈ 96% of the work, as at k 8). Growth k 8 → 9 ≈ 200–400× in nodes.
- **Partial result.** 568,601 type flats (dim 2: 565,140; 3: 3,455; 4: 6) = 81 classes; 10 maximal = #31 (3 × 3), #23, #37, #69, #70 + F35, F54, F56, F57, F80. #68 not reached in the 2%.
- **History of speedups** (all exact, verified on `--single` dup counts 2,061 / 6,487 / 208,166 for k 5–7): k 8 min-dim 2 went 14,339 s → 1,998 s (`--fast --canon2`) → 1,049 s (`--dedup-min-dim 5`) → 805 s (Fx hasher + mimalloc). ## 5. What has been tried (and what it taught)

1. **Exhaustive subsets of a gamut** (Phase 1): 12.6 B subsets ⇒ 30,065 families; window-dependent.
2. **zgen types + placement** (Phase 2): symbolic cancellation patterns; did not finish at 11–14 notes.
3. **Factor-product search `zprod.py` → `zprod-rs` "ladder"** (Phases 4–12): fast, but completeness depends on bounded factor shapes; misses families when U needs more −1s (above ~5-limit q 2,000). Lower bounds only beyond proven ranges.
4. **Shape-free clique enumeration `zprod-rs --enum`** (Phase 13+): the proof tool for lists; cost grows ~15–20× per q step at 9 notes; ceilings: ≤ 8 odd primes, ≤ 2,048 allowed intervals.
5. **Constructive generators** (`chordgen.py`, `holegen.py`, merges via `zprod-rs`): explain all but 9 proven families; generators are lower bounds.
6. **Universal types `typeflats(.py|-rs)`** (Phase 18 →): settled k 6, 7, 8 (2-D) and k 9 (≥ 3-D). k 9 2-D stalled (§4).
7. **Prune "skip nodes inside a known shape"** (2026-11-27, `prune_test.py`, `tables/shapes9/prune_test.txt`): sound, but **0 of 200** sampled k 9 nodes (dims 2–4) lie inside any of the 11 known shapes (control: 81/81 known type classes detected). Saving < ~1.5%. Do not build it.
8. **Memory/dedup trade-offs:** `--dedup-min-dim` 0/4/5/6 at k 8: D 4 −17% nodes but 26× more stored classes than D 5; D 6 +26% nodes, 36× fewer classes. The k 9 run used D 5 on 188 GB (fine).

## 6. Open directions (ranked by my judgment; none started)

1. **Two-row direct search.** Enumerate 9-note pairs with both sets on two adjacent lattice lines (Z × {0,1}), by span L, as homometric pairs (hash interval multisets; canonical form under translation, mirror, swap, row swap). Complete for two-row shapes up to span L (7 of 9 known 2-D shapes are two-row; widths up to 24 in prime form, F57 spans 0–23). Cheap relative to §4 (Rust, VM). Then check each new class's maximality against the 11 known shapes (`type_image`). Gives a stronger lower bound, not a proof.
2. **Factor-pair search in 2 variables.** Enumerate U, V with coefficients in {−1, 0, 1} (maybe ±2), supports bounded via Newton polytopes (hull(P) = hull(U) + hull(V)), keep U·V and U·V\* with 0/1 coefficients and 9 terms each. Every known 2-D shape fits. Lower bound; tests whether F56/F57-type "both signed" pairs are common.
3. **Make the exact search affordable.** Ideas not yet tested: (a) a necessary condition for a dim-3/4 node to contain any **2-D** type flat (most of the cost is dim 3–4 nodes that yield nothing new); (b) dedup at dim 3–4 with a compact table (memory was the reason for D 5); (c) order/split the frontier by cost and sample a random 1% of frontier ids to get an unbiased cost estimate first (ids are processed in order; early ids may not be typical); (d) bigger machine only if the quota case (b7b28750: N4D 192, C4D 192, all-regions 200 CPUs) was granted — ask the human first.
4. **Theory.** A proof that every 2-D 9-note type has bounded width, or a classification via the factor law for two-row sets (reduces to 1-D problems on two lines) would turn (1) into a proof.
5. Small leftovers: c3 (8-note) first q via the 3^m bound + `new5.py`-style search; #23's factor structure; shapes page factor line per card (`shell/BACKLOG.md` [ratio-rhythm]); paper timeline phases for 2026-11-26/27; land `ratio-rhythm-lib` on `main`.

## 9. Rules from the human (binding)

- **All compute on the VM, never on the PC** (searches, timings, data builds, benchmarks). Small file edits/plots on the PC are fine.
- **Cost first:** before any multi-hour VM run state $/h and the estimated total and ask. Cheapest adequate machine (N4D spot for CPU-bound jobs). Stop the VM when jobs finish. Spot preemption: restart and resume without asking.
- **Only one VM, shared.** If several models work in parallel, coordinate: one heavy job at a time, separate run folders (`~/zruns/<yourname>_<date>/`), never touch `~/zruns/types9d2/state` except to resume that run, never `~/ipcat`.
- **Watchdogs:** a PC-side loop that restarts a stopped VM survives TaskStop and context clears on this Windows box (Git Bash). On 2026-11-27 orphaned watchdogs restarted the VM and the stopped k 9 run twice. Before stopping anything on purpose: `Get-CimInstance Win32_Process | Where-Object { $_.CommandLine -match 'watchdog' }` and kill them; recheck VM status minutes later.
- **Profile before optimizing;** predict before running (e.g. where a new prime first appears); test small first; label lower bounds vs proofs; say what you verified vs inferred.
- **Vocabulary:** a:b:c (chord), 1/(a:b:c) (mirror), A·U, "second factor U"; no tonal names (no major/minor triad, inversion, motif). American spelling. Times in Eastern.
- **Git:** work on a branch off `ratio-rhythm-lib` (e.g. `zpairs-<topic>`), commit with clear messages, push; never push to `main`, never force-push. Don't bulk-format files.
