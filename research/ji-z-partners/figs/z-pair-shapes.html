Z-Pair Shapes

Every pattern we know that two different note sets can share while having exactly the same intervals. A pattern is abstract: pick a ratio for each of its directions and it becomes real JI pairs. The q shown is the smallest intervallic odd limit at which the pattern first occurs in the families found so far; for the F shapes (found by the 9-note flat search) it comes from a search over realizations, and a range means the first q lies in it.

set P set Q (same intervals, different set) grid dots: lattice points in the pattern's own coordinates

Shape view: each shape in its prime form, a fixed rule computed offline: over all changes of basis with integer entries and determinant ±1 (2-D: entries up to 10 in size; 3-D: up to 1), shifting each set, mirroring each set and swapping P and Q, take the drawing with the smallest total width (bounding-box widths of P and Q added up), ties broken by the smallest sorted coordinate list. 2-D shapes are drawn with their second coordinate across. The 4-D 3 × 3 product keeps its A + B coordinates: chord A (small triangle) repeated at each note of chord B for P, and of B mirrored for Q. First appearance view: the smallest real example in the families we have (for the F shapes: the smallest example found by the realization search), on the JI lattice (axes are prime exponents; each set shifted to start at 0); the 7-note lines lie on the chain of fifths.
